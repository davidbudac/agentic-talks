You know the chat window. That's maybe 10% of the story.

Basics · pricing literacy

First, learn to read an AI price tag

1 · Subscription

Flat $/month, capped by usage limits. The labs: free → ~$20 → $100–200. Simple, predictable.

2 · Credits

Buy a bucket; each image/clip/minute burns some. Most creative tools. Hardest to budget — retries burn credits too.

3 · Per-seat

$/user/month for teams — adds admin controls, SSO, "no training on your data". Usually ~$20–40/seat.

4 · API (per token)

Metered pay-per-use for building software on top. No cap; scales with usage.

And the trap to remember: free tier ≠ free for commercial use. More on that in "the fine print". All prices in this deck: July 2026 snapshot — verify before quoting.

5
Basics · chatbot vs agent

A chatbot answers. An agent acts.

Chatbot — the one you know

You ask → it tells you what to do → you open the files, do the steps, paste results back… and repeat.

Agent

You ask → it does the work itself — reads the files, takes the steps, checks the result → you review at the end.

Often it's the same model underneath. The difference is everything wrapped around it — and every tool in this talk is some flavour of that wrapping.

6
Basics · the loop

An agent is a loop

  • ① the model proposes an action → ② the tool runs it → ③ the result comes back. Repeat until ④ done.
  • Like a cook: read the recipe → do a step → taste → adjust → repeat.
  • This loop is the whole "magic" of agentic AI — no magic, just disciplined repetition.

Every agent product in this deck — Cowork, Codex, agent mode, all of them — is this loop in a different costume.

7
Basics · the brain

The model has no memory

  • A model is autocomplete on steroids — it only ever predicts the next word. Text in, text out. No database, no "you".
  • Think of the guy in Memento — the film hero who can't form new memories, living off notes & tattoos: each interaction, the model has no idea what came before.
  • So the tools around it must re-send everything it should know, every single time.

Keep this in mind — it explains why tools plug into your files, your brand kit, your inbox: they're the model's notes & tattoos.

In practice · worked example

Monday's deck, step by step

you › "build the Q2 review deck from this folder, same structure as Q1" …the agent, on its own: ✓ read the folder 14 files — results CSV, Q1 deck, brand template ✓ proposed a structure 12 slides, mirroring Q1 — waits for your OK ✓ built Q2-review.pptx charts from the CSV, your template & fonts ⚠ flagged two gaps "no June numbers for channel X — placeholder" you › add the June export… review, tweak two slides, done ✓

Notice what it didn't do: invent the missing numbers. And notice your role — you moved from making the deck to directing & reviewing it.

In practice · deep research

Deep research: one question, a full report

  • Ask one question → the AI browses dozens of sources for 5–30 minutes → returns a multi-page, cited report.
  • Shipped by all three labs (and Perplexity) under roughly this name; included from the ~$20 tiers.
  • Ideal for: market landscapes, competitor teardowns, "what does the research actually say?"
Looks right ≠ is right

These reports look authoritative and still contain errors. Treat them as a well-organised starting point with a reading list — never a finished document you forward.

23
In practice · the rule

AI drafts. You sign.

Review scales
with risk

An internal brainstorm needs a skim. Anything public, legal or financial needs a real review. The tool drafts fast precisely so that you can afford to review well.

  • Every tool in this deck assumes a human checkpoint — that's what makes them safe to adopt.
  • The failure mode isn't "AI made a mistake" — it's "nobody looked."
  • So: which tool for which job? That's the rest of this talk →

Landscape · hyperframes

HyperFrames — video as code

A presenter who never books a studio

Landscape · the fine print

Before you use any of it for real

① Licence

Free tier ≠ commercial use. ElevenLabs, Suno, Ideogram: the commercial licence starts at the first paid tier.

② Privacy

Cheap tiers are often public by default — Midjourney below Pro shows your prompts & images to everyone. No unreleased work.

③ Provenance

Where did the training data come from? Firefly & ElevenLabs Music are licensed — the defensible picks. Others are in litigation.

④ Budget

Credit pricing is slippery — retries burn credits. Start on fixed plans, auto-top-up off.

And once more: every price in this deck is a July 2026 snapshot. Check the vendor's page before you commit a budget.

Five wild stories

True, documented, recent — what agents actually did out in the world. Good and bad.

37
Stories · 1 of 5

Claude ran a shop — and went broke

  • Anthropic's Project Vend: Claude ran a real snack shop in the office — buying stock, setting prices, chatting with customers on Slack.
  • Month one: −$200. Employees sweet-talked it into discounts, it hallucinated inventory — and once ordered tungsten cubes because someone asked.
  • Phase two — proper tools, a CRM, guardrails, an "AI CEO" — and the losing weeks largely disappeared.
Powerful
and naive

The raw model was both at once. What turned it from a pushover into a business? Tools and guardrails — the thesis of this entire deck.

38
Stories · 2 of 5

Ten years of research. Two days.

10 yrs → 2 days

Imperial College London spent a decade on how superbugs acquire resistance. Google's AI co-scientist, given the same question, ranked the correct — unpublished — answer first in two days.

  • It also proposed a hypothesis the team had never considered. The researchers themselves went on record — this isn't vendor marketing.
  • The honest caveat: the AI generated the hypothesis. Humans had spent the decade doing the lab work that proved it.
  • The point: it didn't replace ten years of science — it means the next question might not need them.
39
Stories · 3 of 5

Gold medals, in minutes

CFA Level III

The hardest finance exam — essays included. Models passed a full mock in minutes (best ~79%). Humans train ~300 hours per level.

Maths Olympiad gold

Official IMO gold medal, certified by the organisers — 5 of 6 problems, standard conditions. Only ~1 in 10 human contestants got gold.

The reframe: the exam-shaped part of professional work — recall, procedure, structured answers — is now cheap. Judgment, accountability, and knowing what to ask are not.

40
Stories · 4 of 5

The same skill, both directions

🛡 Defence

Google's Big Sleep agent found a flaw in software on billions of devices — one only attackers knew — and it was patched before they could strike. A first.

⚔ Offence

Anthropic disclosed the first largely AI-orchestrated espionage campaign: a jailbroken coding agent ran ~80–90% of the attack autonomously (Nov 2025).

It's the same loop from slide 6 doing both jobs. Which one it does is decided by the humans and guardrails around it — which is why the fine-print slides in this deck aren't decoration.

41
Stories · 5 of 5

The best AI ad of 2026 was made by humans

  • Anthropic's Super Bowl debut — "Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude." — was made the old-fashioned way: human agency, human director, real actors.
  • Result: daily active users +11%, Claude into the top-10 free iPhone apps.
  • Meanwhile Coca-Cola's AI-generated Christmas ad reportedly took ~70,000 generated clips and ~100 people to assemble two finished spots.
Floor, not ceiling

AI raised the floor — what a small team can produce. It hasn't raised the ceiling. Craft, taste and a real idea still win.

Plugging in · mcp

MCP = "USB-C for AI"

  • Before: a custom connector for every AI × every tool. MCP is the standard plug: build once, works everywhere.
  • Anthropic open-sourced it (Nov 2024) → OpenAI, Google & Microsoft adopted it (2025) → donated to the Linux Foundation (Dec 2025). 10,000+ servers exist.
  • Every "connector" or "app" on the next slides is this, under the hood. You never see it — it just makes the plugs fit.

Plugging in · claude × office

Claude inside Excel, PowerPoint & Word

  • A side panel inside the real apps — not copy-paste from a chat tab.
  • Excel: builds & fixes models without breaking formulas, tracks every change per cell · PowerPoint: decks in your template · Word: tracked changes · Outlook: beta.
  • Install from the Microsoft add-in marketplace · included in any paid Claude plan (~$20+) · needs a work/school Microsoft 365 account.
One thread,
four apps

Plugging in · files from chat

Real files, straight from the chat

No add-in needed

Claude & ChatGPT both produce genuine .pptx / .xlsx / .docx in the chat: describe it, upload source data, download an editable file — not text to reformat.

Which one when?

Add-ins (last slide) work inside a file you already have. Chat produces a new file. Editing the Q2 model → add-in. First draft of the deck → chat.

For many people this is the biggest single upgrade in this talk — and it needs nothing installed. Included from the ~$20 tiers.
