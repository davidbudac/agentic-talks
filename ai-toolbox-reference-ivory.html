Words you'll keep hearing

  • TokenA chunk of text ≈ ¾ of a word. What models read & bill in.
  • PromptThe text you send the model.
  • ContextEverything the model sees in one call — its short-term memory.
  • AgentAn AI that does things in a loop, not just answers.
  • HarnessThe program wrapped around the model — its hands.
  • ConnectorA plug between an AI and another app (Gmail, Drive, Slack…).
  • MCPThe open standard those plugs are built on — "USB-C for AI".
  • CreditsPre-paid units most creative tools bill in — each generation burns some.
  • Generative AIAI that makes media — images, video, voice, music.
  • Deep researchThe AI browses for 5–30 min and returns a cited report.

Don't memorise these — each comes back with its own slide. This is just your map.

Claude Code & Codex — the coding agents

Claude Code

Anthropic's agent for developers: lives in the terminal or editor, reads a whole codebase, writes & runs code, fixes its own mistakes. Included from Pro (~$20/mo).

Codex

OpenAI's equivalent, on GPT‑5.6 — terminal, IDE and a cloud version that works on tasks while you're away. Bundled into ChatGPT plans (full access from Plus, ~$20/mo).

Why you should care even if you never code: these transformed programming first — Anthropic says over 80% of its own production code is now written by Claude. Then someone asked: what if we removed the code part? →

In practice · deep research · Reference · July 2026 snapshot · Back to the talk

Deep research: one question, a full report

  • Ask one question → the AI browses dozens of sources for 5–30 minutes → returns a multi-page, cited report.
  • Shipped by all three labs (and Perplexity) under roughly this name; included from the ~$20 tiers.
  • Ideal for: market landscapes, competitor teardowns, "what does the research actually say?"

Looks right ≠ is right

These reports look authoritative and still contain errors. Treat them as a well-organised starting point with a reading list — never a finished document you forward.

A presenter who never books a studio

Synthesia — corporate

Script → presenter in 160+ languages. Training & explainers you re-render, not re-shoot, when things change. Free 10 min/mo · Starter $29 · Creator $89/mo.

HeyGen — marketing

Its party trick: video translation with lip-sync — the original speaker appears to speak another language, mouth matched. Free 3 videos · from ~$29/mo.

D-ID is the budget option (~$16/mo): animates a still photo into a talking head. Rule of thumb: Synthesia for training, HeyGen for marketing & localisation. Prices July 2026.

Soundtracks & the sleeper hit

Suno

Describe a song → finished track with vocals. Pro ~$10/mo incl. commercial rights — but training data is in litigation: legal check before paid media.

Udio

Settled with the labels; rebuilding as a licensed "walled garden" — downloads currently disabled. A tool that can't export isn't a tool yet.

ElevenLabs Music

Licensed catalogue (Merlin/Kobalt, royalties paid) — the legally safest AI music route.

Descript — edit video like a document

It transcribes your recording; delete a sentence in the text and it's cut from the video. One-click "um" removal, studio-sound cleanup. ~$16–24/mo — the highest ROI per hour of learning in this entire deck.

The category the chatbots ate

Jasper

The old category king — revenue roughly halved. Survives on brand-voice consistency across big teams. ~$39–69/mo.

Copy.ai

Left the category — pivoted to sales automation. Agents from ~$249/mo. The pivot tells the story.

Writer

The governance play: flags copy that breaks brand or compliance rules before human review. ~$39/user · enterprise.

AI over your own wiki — "summarise every campaign retro mentioning X." Now needs Business, ~$20/user/mo.

Landscape · research & search · Reference · July 2026 snapshot · Back to the talk

Perplexity — search that shows its sources

  • Ask a question → an answer in prose with citations to live sources — every claim verifiable, not ten blue links.
  • Pro ~$20/mo: unlimited pro search + 20 deep-research runs a day. Enterprise ~$40/seat.
  • Comet — its browser with the assistant built in — went free worldwide in October 2025 (before that, gated behind the $200 Max tier).

Careful where it clicks

An AI browser acting on pages you're logged into is a documented prompt-injection risk — a hidden instruction on a page can steer it. Keep agentic browsers away from sensitive logged-in systems.

Prices ~July 2026.

Stories · supporting example · Reference · July 2026 snapshot · Back to the talk

AI proposed a hypothesis. Scientists supplied the evidence.

Hypothesis
→ validation

Imperial College London spent a decade on how superbugs acquire resistance. Google's AI co-scientist, given the same question, ranked the correct — unpublished — answer first in two days.

  • It also proposed a hypothesis the team had never considered. The researchers themselves went on record — this isn't vendor marketing.
  • The honest caveat: the AI generated the hypothesis. Humans had spent the decade doing the lab work that proved it.
  • A generated hypothesis still needs experimental validation.
Stories · supporting example · Reference · July 2026 snapshot · Back to the talk

Gold medals, in minutes

CFA Level III

The hardest finance exam — essays included. Models passed a full mock in minutes (best ~79%). Humans train ~300 hours per level.

Maths Olympiad gold

Official IMO gold medal, certified by the organisers — 5 of 6 problems, standard conditions. Only ~1 in 10 human contestants got gold.

The reframe: the exam-shaped part of professional work — recall, procedure, structured answers — is now cheap. Judgment, accountability, and knowing what to ask are not.

The same skill, both directions

Defence

Google's Big Sleep agent found a flaw in software on billions of devices — one only attackers knew — and it was patched before they could strike. A first.

Offence

Anthropic disclosed the first largely AI-orchestrated espionage campaign: a jailbroken coding agent ran ~80–90% of the attack autonomously (Nov 2025).

It's the same loop introduced in the main talk doing both jobs. Which one it does is decided by the humans and guardrails around it — which is why the fine-print slides in this deck aren't decoration.

Connectors

Plugging in · the built-ins · Reference · July 2026 snapshot · Back to the talk

Or use what's already in the building

Microsoft 365 Copilot

Agent mode in Word, Excel & PowerPoint — works on the document itself. ~$21–30/user/mo on top of M365. Fun fact: the Office agents run on Anthropic's models.

Gemini in Workspace

Now bundled into base plans (from ~$14/user) — Gmail, Docs, Meet. Star feature: =AI("prompt", A2) in Sheets — an AI prompt as a formula, filled down 1,000 rows.

The trade-off vs the add-ins: built-ins are the path of least resistance (and often the easiest compliance story) — the dedicated tools are usually better at the work itself. Prices July 2026.

Plugging in · the universal adapter · Reference · July 2026 snapshot · Back to the talk

The universal adapter for everything else

  • No official connector for your tool? Zapier MCP: one connection exposes ~30,000 actions across 8,000+ apps to Claude or ChatGPT.
  • Setup is paste-a-URL — then just say it: "add this lead to the CRM and post a summary to the channel."
  • Make offers the same on its visual canvas. Both need their own (cheap) plan + a paid AI plan.

Expose the minimum

The safety rule: plug in only the actions you need. The AI can only misuse what you've connected — keep the adapter's keyring short.
